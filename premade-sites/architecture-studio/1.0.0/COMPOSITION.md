# Common Ground: selected Index direction

Visual thesis: a quiet working portfolio, with an architectural project index, full scenes, and readable matter-of-fact writing on warm paper.

The user selected C / Index. Preserve its project-led opening rather than replacing it with a different full-bleed direction. Native navigation disclosure, clear hover/focus states, and project-to-detail navigation provide interaction; ornamental entrances or scroll effects would add little and are omitted under the native-only policy.

## Page composition and rejection pass

- Home: retain the selected directory beside the courtyard scene; one contrasting reading-room record establishes range, then a concise practice introduction and contact link. The directory has a real navigation job. Avoid repeated marketing split sections.
- Projects: a compact introduction followed by two consistent, editable project records. Text identifies the study and design decision; imagery lets visitors compare enclosure and adaptive reuse. Links lead to the courtyard case study or a reading-room inquiry. Repetition serves comparison.
- Project Detail: title and brief, a wide courtyard scene, then a narrow case-study reading column with three specific decisions. No invented dimensions, client, awards, construction date, or completion claim. A return link reconnects to the portfolio.
- About: a text-led practice statement followed by a three-part working approach. No fictional portraits or made-up team biographies. The story explains a sample practice's values without pretending it is an operating firm.
- Services: a vertical service scope and deliverables list, with a separate preparation checklist. No identical pricing cards, fake packages, process numerals, or unsupported booking controls. The inquiry link is the primary action.
- Contact: a concise project brief followed by the native form and a short next-step explanation. Form remains explicitly unconfigured until a receiving endpoint is connected and tested. No fake contact address or submission success.

All pages use the same 1200px content container, native shared header/footer, 14px minimum text, and shared text/color tokens. Project images repeat only when identifying the same study across portfolio and detail views. Agent rationale here is not visual acceptance evidence; final rendered review remains separate.
