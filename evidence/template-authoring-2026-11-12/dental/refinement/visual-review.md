# Dental homepage refinement r30 visual review

## Scope and evidence

Reviewed all r30 homepage viewport tiles at 390, 768, 1024, and 1440 px (32 tiles). Read `Final/checks.json`, the r24 feedback map, current home/source changes, and the updated layout avoid list. Compared the r30 FAQ/closing and first-visit panel with the available r24 captures. The audit reports one H1, loaded images, and no horizontal overflow at all four widths.

## Findings against the seven comments

1. The first-visit and care-team actions now read as distinct: the visit panel uses white type and a white button on the blue panel; the team section uses a blue button on white. The CTA styles are visibly differentiated.
2. Contained editorial photos use consistent rounded corners in the reviewed approach section. The wide team scene and the final CTA background remain full-bleed as intentional section backgrounds; their square edges read as deliberate, not an inconsistent card treatment.
3. The approach image occupies a balanced column beside its stacked heading and supporting copy. The source sets the image to 380 px desktop height and 300 px mobile height, with 340 px tablet height. The copy remains grouped at all widths.
4. Three care-category icons are visible in their corresponding sections and use consistent blue line styling.
5. The first-visit panel now has a white first step against the blue surface; its three static step cards are distinct and readable at desktop, tablet, and phone widths.
6. The final CTA is a full-width clinic image with a dark blue overlay. Text and white action remain legible across the reviewed widths, and this treatment gives the close a clear visual identity.
7. The appointment-confirmation FAQ and the extra appointment disclaimer beneath the CTA are absent in r30. The FAQ has a practical plain-language question instead.

No visual defects found in the inspected captures. This is a screenshot review, not an interaction or keyboard audit. The 768 px tablet menu is present in capture data; its open/close behavior was not tested here. Catalog approval remains separate.
